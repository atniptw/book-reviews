# The Tainted Cup (Shadow of the Leviathan, Books 1–3)

**Author:** Robert Jackson Bennett

**Rating:** 4/5

**Review Date:** 2026-08-18

**Format:** Audiobook

**Reading Context:** Before bed, and on walks

---

## Review

Shadow of the Leviathan holds up well as a trilogy, even though it drops off in book three. The Tainted Cup and A Drop of Corruption are both excellent — inventive magic powers with real trade-offs, a genuinely engaging mystery, and Ana and Din are a great pairing throughout all three books. A Trade of Blood is where it lost me a bit: the pacing and plot loosened up, and the murder mystery that anchored the first two books took a backseat. Each book reads well in isolation but clearly builds on the last, and across the whole series I could rarely solve the mystery before the reveal — it consistently gave me less to work with than I wanted.

## What I Liked

- Inventive magic powers with meaningful trade-offs
- Ana and Din's dynamic stayed strong across all three books
- Each book stands well on its own while still building on the last
- Audiobook narration

## What I Disliked

- Mystery rarely gave enough clues to solve before the reveal, especially books 1-2
- A Trade of Blood's pacing and plot loosened up, and the mystery took a backseat
- Book 1's descriptions of unpleasant food/eating were sometimes overly graphic

## Additional Notes

Best entry point into Bennett's mystery-fantasy side — if you liked Foundryside's world-building, this pairs the same strength with a tighter mystery hook, at least for the first two books. Rated as one series entry at 4/5; individually more like 5/5/3, book to book.
